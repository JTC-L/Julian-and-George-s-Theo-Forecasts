Order book replay (set 2)

Bar width is cumulative size from the best price outward (the Total column), scaled to the larger side. Age is the age_s value from the book file; a dot marks a level with age 0. Hover a level to see its weight, which is size × 2−(% from mid) × e−(k·age)².

Book theo. Every price is multiplied by the total weight of the opposite side. With bids 8.30, 8.35, 8.40 and asks 8.45, 8.50, 8.55, each of 8.45, 8.50 and 8.55 is multiplied by (weight of 8.30 + weight of 8.35 + weight of 8.40), and each of 8.30, 8.35 and 8.40 by (weight of 8.45 + weight of 8.50 + weight of 8.55). The theo is the sum of all those products divided by the sum of the multipliers used. Level weight = size × 2−(% from mid) × e−(k·age)², with k = 1/(40√2), so the time weight's inflection is at 40 s. A level's own weight therefore only matters through its side's total, and each price counts equally. With one level each side and no decay this is the 99 / 101 example.

Last trade in the theo. This is a modelling step, not part of the book: a trade is a price taken, not a market inserted, so the ladder above always shows the book as given. When the switch is on, the theo counts the most recent trade printed by the selected snapshot as one more order on the aggressor's side: a buy counts with the bids and a sell with the asks. It is treated as 0 seconds old, so its time weight is 1, and its price is taken as midway between the price they traded at and the next level in the direction they traded. For a buy that is the next ask above, and for a sell the next bid below, read from the selected snapshot's book (one tick further out if the visible book has no such level). It adds to a level's weight if one sits at that price, otherwise it counts as an extra price on that side. Either way its weight, size × 2−(% from mid), goes into that side's total. Mid, spread, the distance weights and the prices traded in the simulation all use the book as given. Switch it off to see the theo without it.

Prediction: earlier theos vs mid now

Each error is a forecast of the mid at Tn, made k snapshots earlier, minus the mid that actually printed at Tn, so closer to zero is better. The theo forecast uses the theo at Tn−k. The mid-change comparison uses the mid at Tn−k. Looking back two and three snapshots checks whether the theo was early with a good idea. The theo is the one shown in the book panel, so it follows the last-trade switch. The theo wins a snapshot when its error is smaller in size than the mid-change error.

Trigger and cumulative PnL

PnL marked to mid PnL marked to theo

Trigger: at each snapshot, if the theo is above ask levels, buy them, and if it is below bid levels, sell into them, working outward from the best price, part-filling the last level once the cap is reached. Size cap: when entering or adding to a position (buying when flat or long, selling when flat or short), trade at most 100 shares or 20% of the shares showing on the side traded, whichever is smaller. When getting out (buying when short, selling when long), the flat cap is 100. The position is capped at ±300: buying stops at +300 and selling stops at −300, and an entering trade is cut back so the position never passes the limit. If the position is at the limit and the theo sits between the best bid and best ask, trade up to 100 shares at the touch to cut exposure: sell when long, buy when short. Prices are the displayed ones, from the book as given, not the synthetic last-trade level, and the 20% is taken on that visible book too. There is no exit rule beyond this, and no fees. PnL = cash + position × mark, with the mark being that snapshot's mid or its theo. Each snapshot is treated as tradable, but resting orders persist between snapshots, so the same liquidity can be counted more than once.
